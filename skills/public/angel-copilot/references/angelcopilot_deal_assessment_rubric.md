# AngelCopilot Deal Assessment Rubric

## Seven factors

Assess all seven factors. The default weights remain stable so companies can be compared. Product measures customer value and performance today; Defensibility measures the durability of the advantage.

| Factor | Weight | Score 1 anchor | Score 3 anchor | Score 5 anchor |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Team | 25% | Material execution or integrity weaknesses | Relevant team with gaps and some execution evidence | Exceptional founder-market fit and independently supported execution; key roles covered |
| Market | 20% | Weak urgency, inaccessible demand or adverse timing | Clear customer need and reachable wedge, with competitive constraints | Strong urgent demand, accessible buyers and compelling timing, supported by specific evidence |
| Product | 15% | Poor customer value or material technical weakness | Working product addresses a clear problem; performance remains partly unproven | Independently demonstrated advantage and reliable delivery for the company's stage |
| Traction | 15% | Weak or deteriorating customer validation | Credible paid adoption or stage-appropriate pilot evidence | Exceptional repeatable demand, retention and conversion for the company's stage |
| Unit Economics | 10% | Structurally unattractive delivery economics | Plausible economics with cost and scalability questions | Fully supported margins, acquisition/delivery costs and capital needs consistent with scaling |
| Defensibility | 10% | Advantage is readily copied or structurally weak | Plausible moat mechanism that is not yet compounding | Evidence that switching costs, IP, data or distribution advantages are compounding |
| Terms | 5% | Price or rights materially undermine the investor's net payoff | Reasonable price and understandable security with trade-offs | Attractive price and security economics; transparent fees, conversion and investor protections |

Use 0–5 with at most one decimal. Scores 2 and 4 interpolate between anchors; 0 is reserved for demonstrated extreme weakness, never absence of information. Score all seven factors provisionally when the available materials and the company's stage/business model provide a reasonable basis. Pitch decks and AngelList summaries are expected to omit diligence detail; missing disclosure alone is not a reason to leave a factor or the overall score blank, and it is not evidence that the company performs poorly. Assess the business model and stage-appropriate signals, state assumptions and what could change the score, and use Low evidence strength when support is mostly reported or incomplete. Lower a factor for substantive risks supported by the evidence or business model, not as an automatic penalty for missing documents. Reserve null / Not Scored with Unknown evidence strength for the rare factor where no responsible provisional judgment is possible, and explain why. Calculate the weighted average over scored factors using their effective weights; show coverage and name any genuinely unscored factor. Unknown factors are omitted, not treated as zero. Say Not calculated only if no factor can be scored. Archived v1–v3 score handling remains unchanged. Keep Evidence strength (High / Medium / Low / Unknown) distinct from company quality: it describes support for the judgment, not a verbal translation of the score.

Anchor evidence to stage and business model: pre-seed customer experiments differ from seed retention; hardware needs reliability, yield, warranty and working-capital evidence; biotech needs scientific, regulatory and manufacturing milestones. Avoid demanding mature SaaS metrics from every startup. A preference mismatch belongs in investor suitability, not a fabricated low company score.

Apply supported `evaluation_weight_overrides`, merge with defaults and normalize to 100%. Show effective weights. These are preference weights, not predictive coefficients.

## One action recommendation

Every assessment gives one action recommendation at the current terms. Do not ask the user to choose screening or commitment mode, and do not produce separate verdicts.

- **INVEST:** recommend a normal cheque now only when stage-appropriate diligence is sufficient, terms and net economics are understood and attractive, investor fit is compatible, and the cheque is justified within the investor's profile range, subscription minimum and known budget. Apply a configured personal return hurdle. Accepted venture risks may remain.
- **PURSUE:** prioritize diligence now because the thesis, stage-appropriate signals and current price make the work worthwhile for this investor. Explain the concrete reason and next diligence action. No cheque is recommended yet.
- **WAIT:** do not prioritize diligence now. State a specific evidence item, milestone, timing event or changed term that would cause the investor to reopen the opportunity. Do not use WAIT merely because diligence is incomplete when resolving that gap is worth doing; that is PURSUE. Do not use WAIT as a synonym for no cheque when an INVEST/PURSUE/PASS action is clearer.
- **PASS:** stop work at these terms or for this investor. Give a concrete reason. Fatal issues, unattractive net economics or incompatible investor constraints require PASS, even if the company has strengths.

**PURSUE score gate:** recommend PURSUE only when the weighted score is at least **3.5 / 5** and there is a specific reason to spend diligence time at current terms. Below 3.5, or with no calculable score, use WAIT at most; PASS remains appropriate when further work is unlikely to change the conclusion. A score of 3.5 or higher is eligibility, not an automatic PURSUE. The score is descriptive and does not authorize a cheque. INVEST also requires the explicit diligence, economics, suitability and cheque checks above. Do not append a conditional investment view.

Missing private evidence, cheque preferences or a probability return model do not automatically lower the score or make a deal WAIT. Judge the investment case and the value of further work, not document completeness. Do not pursue a deal solely to obtain information routinely available in diligence. An unresolved investment-critical question means PURSUE when it is worthwhile to resolve; WAIT when the opportunity should not receive time until a named trigger occurs.

Use priority high / medium / low to show follow-up urgency. INVEST uses high; PURSUE uses high or medium; PASS uses low. WAIT may use any priority, but identify what is being monitored. Show **Attention: Yes** for INVEST and high-priority PURSUE, unless a fatal issue, unresolved hard risk or incompatible constraint makes follow-up inappropriate. For archived version 2 records, retain the historic attention heuristic for INVEST and WAIT with score >=3.9 and profile fit >=0.5, subject to its original gates. Attention highlights work priority; it is not authorization to invest.

A fatal issue yields PASS; an unresolved investment-critical blocker prevents INVEST. Explain the materiality of each blocker. A risk accepted at this stage is not a blocker simply because it remains uncertain: preclinical efficacy, future flight milestones, scaled CAC or a mature moat may be the very risks the cheque funds. If diligence is worth doing to determine whether the risk is acceptable, recommend PURSUE. If the investor should wait for the company to reach a milestone before spending time, recommend WAIT and name the milestone. Lack of public private-company data is not evidence of fraud.

Review applicable company and syndicate terms; ordinary unsigned investor subscription paperwork is not itself a blocker. Unknown material costs or rights require clarification, but modest bounded costs may be stress-tested with explicit assumptions. Distinguish private primary evidence (contracts, collections, customer references) from unsupported assertions; it need not appear on the public web to be useful.

Use cheque minimum, typical and maximum only from the investor profile. Select a justified amount within that range only for INVEST, including subscription minimum and known budget. Explain departures from the typical cheque. Without a complete range, do not recommend INVEST or size a personal cheque; continue assessing whether diligence is worthwhile and show cheque-independent economics. Quote all-in cash outlay including entry expenses; convert a deployed-principal minimum to that basis. Never invent budget capacity.

Follow-on participation is not part of default sizing or economics. Lack of pro-rata is not itself a blocker for an investor who rarely follows on. Discuss later participation only when requested or material to this security.

## Deal economics

Use supportable ranges for post-money entry valuation, future dilution, expenses and carry, distinguishing reported terms from assumed stress inputs. State a target net cash multiple and calculate the distributable equity exit needed to reach it. No personal cheque or probability estimate is needed. Show lower, midpoint and higher required exits with the associated inputs, not an expected-return forecast. Explain whether the company could plausibly reach that scale; do not infer attractiveness merely because the arithmetic is valid. If conversion or preference assumptions prevent even a credible range, explain the limitation without automatically changing the recommendation. Economic sensitivity alone does not justify INVEST.

## Net return stress tests

For a supportable optional full model, use one explicitly configured normal cheque with no follow-ons. Use four distinct outcomes: loss / zero recovery, bear / partial recovery, base and upside. Probabilities are subjective assumptions, sum to 100%, and the zero-recovery case must have positive probability. State the source or reasoning for valuation and outcome assumptions; do not invent probabilities that look statistically calibrated.

Disclose total cash outlay, deployable capital, fee rate and whether fees are deducted or added, post-money entry valuation, currency conversion, illustrative entry ownership, future dilution and exit ownership, carry rate and its capital-return basis, horizon, and excluded taxes/additional expenses. SAFE cap-based ownership is only an illustration, not a guaranteed conversion price. Exit values must represent equity value distributable to the modeled holding after the assumed debt and preference waterfall; disclose simplifications. Unknown conversion, senior claims or cost terms may make a credible model unavailable.

Calculate net proceeds, net MOIC and annualized return for each scenario from those inputs. With one outflow and one terminal receipt, annualized return = net MOIC^(1/years) − 1; actual intermediate cash flows require a cash-flow model. Show probability of total loss separately from probability of returning less than cash paid. Show assumption-weighted proceeds alongside their reliance on the upside case and a price sensitivity. Do not headline “expected IRR”; annualizing expected proceeds and averaging scenario annualized returns are different operations and neither is a forecast.

If a personal hurdle is supplied, identify its basis. The optional `base_case_return_hurdle` profile field compares the base case's net annualized return, not an expected IRR. If no hurdle exists, identify the decision as a qualitative investment judgment and do not claim a personal hurdle was met. Leave the probability model unavailable when inputs cannot credibly be specified. PURSUE / WAIT / PASS remain possible without a full probability model; INVEST requires credible net economics supported by reviewed range sensitivities or a full net model, plus sufficient material evidence and understood terms. If a personal base-case hurdle is supplied, a full base-case net model is required to compare it.
