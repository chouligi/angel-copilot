# Assessment reporting contract (version 4)

Use this contract for conversational assessments and generated Markdown, HTML and PDF reports. All formats share the same recommendation meanings, section order, scorecard labels and evidence definitions. Layout can vary; meaning cannot. Never treat illustrative numbers as facts.

## Memo reading order

1. **Company Overview:** open with one plain-language paragraph explaining the product, intended customer, problem, how it works, revenue model and current product status where supported. Aim for 60–100 words, fewer when evidence is sparse. Attribute reported claims, cite sources and state gaps without inventing details. Explain the business before assessing it. For saved assessments without a summary, use only recorded product/customer descriptions or say no description was recorded.
2. **Decision Brief:** show the weighted score as the primary visual callout directly below the heading: large bold `3.8 / 5` in PDF/HTML (28pt in print, larger than the recommendation banner), or `**Weighted score: 3.8 / 5**` on its own line in chat/Markdown. Calculate the weighted average across assessable factors, renormalized by their combined effective weights. Show score coverage separately; it is the sum of weights for scored factors, not the fraction of factors. Name unscored factors when coverage is below 100%; they are excluded, not treated as zero. Say Not calculated only when no positively weighted factor can be scored. Explain that the score is descriptive, not a return prediction or recommendation. Then show **one action recommendation** (INVEST / PURSUE / WAIT / PASS), priority, Priority follow-up: Yes/No and reason, concise rationale and next action. Include stage, instrument, terms and evidence date without repeating the company overview. Develop the four-part Investment Case and Decision: investment case, supporting evidence, counterarguments, and why this recommendation/what could change it. Include a cheque only for INVEST; show “No cheque recommended” for PURSUE, WAIT and PASS, without a displayed zero-currency amount. Do not add a conditional investment view.
3. **Seven-Factor Assessment:** columns are exactly Category, Weight, Score / 5, Evidence strength, Key driver. Show effective weights, concise drivers and 40–70-word category notes with citations. Use only High / Medium / Low / Unknown for Evidence strength. Include this definition in every format: “Evidence strength: High = key claims independently corroborated by authoritative evidence; Medium = some corroboration, with material gaps; Low = evidence is mostly reported, incomplete or conflicting; Unknown = evidence quality could not be assessed. Unknown is not a negative score.” Evidence strength describes support for a rating; it is not a translation of quality. Score factors provisionally using evidence appropriate to company stage and business model. Missing detail alone does not make a factor unassessable or prove poor quality. State assumptions and gaps; use Low when a reasonable provisional judgment rests on reported or incomplete information. Reserve Unknown / Not scored for a factor with no responsible basis for judgment.
4. **Decision-Critical Diligence:** up to five ranked issues. Each states finding, decision impact, evidence needed, resolution type and horizon, and a verifiable reconsideration condition. Distinguish verification now, accepted venture risk and future milestones. Explain why each blocker is material. Routine unsigned investor subscription paperwork is not a blocker; identify the unresolved term.
5. **Top Questions for the Founder:** at most three decision-changing asks linked to issues and evidence routes. Three is a ceiling, not a quota. Route questions for the syndicate lead, counsel or customer to optional follow-ups. For PASS, ask only if an answer could reopen the case. Do not combine unrelated requests into one question.
6. **Deal Economics:** show supported valuation/fee/carry/dilution ranges and the distributable equity exit needed for a target net multiple. Explain assumptions and plausibility without personal cheque sizing or probabilities. An optional full net model uses loss/bear/base/upside stress cases, explicit ownership and costs, no follow-ons and separate total/partial-loss probabilities. If neither is available, explain why. Missing probability modeling or ticket preferences does not itself change the action recommendation. Do not claim a personal hurdle was met when none exists.
7. **Evidence Appendix:** dated sources, actual process completion, evidence and extraction limitations, document inventory, optional follow-ups by audience, and decision/quality checks. Full source paths belong in JSON. Preserve archived questions as unranked; do not manufacture priorities or verification.

Aim for 2–4 main pages plus evidence; clarity takes precedence over filling sections. Batch overviews show Company, Weighted score / 5, score coverage, Recommendation / Priority, Priority follow-up, Decisive reason and Next action. Order by recommendation, then priority (high before medium before low), then evidence strength and weighted score. A medium-priority PURSUE still merits diligence; Priority follow-up: No only means it is not highlighted for priority follow-up. The score is an eligibility gate for PURSUE, not a sufficient reason to pursue; explain the deal-specific reason.

## The single action recommendation

- **INVEST:** sufficient stage-appropriate diligence, understood and attractive net economics, compatible investor constraints, and justified cheque sizing support a normal cheque now. A configured personal return hurdle must be met.
- **PURSUE:** the thesis, stage-appropriate signals and current terms justify prioritizing diligence now. Explain the concrete reason and next diligence action. No cheque is recommended yet.
- **WAIT:** do not prioritize diligence now. Name the evidence, milestone, timing event or changed term that would justify reopening. Do not use WAIT merely because diligence is incomplete when resolving the gap is worthwhile; that is PURSUE.
- **PASS:** stop work at these terms or for this investor, with a concrete reason. Fatal issues, unattractive economics or incompatible investor constraints require PASS.

PURSUE requires a weighted score >=3.5 / 5 and a specific reason to spend diligence time. Below 3.5 or with no score, use WAIT at most; PASS remains appropriate when more work is unlikely to change the conclusion. A score >=3.5 only makes PURSUE eligible. INVEST requires its own evidence, economics, fit and cheque checks; the score alone never authorizes a cheque. Missing private evidence or a probability model does not automatically lower the score or make a deal WAIT. Do not pursue solely to obtain information routinely available in diligence.

Priority is high / medium / low. INVEST uses high; PURSUE uses high or medium; PASS uses low. WAIT may use any priority, but say what is being monitored. Priority follow-up highlights INVEST and high-priority PURSUE unless a fatal issue, unresolved hard risk or incompatible constraint makes follow-up inappropriate. Priority follow-up is not investment authorization.

## Version 4 structured fields

- `schema_version`: 4. New assessments have one `decision.verdict`; there is no assessment mode or second screening/commitment verdict.
- `decision.verdict`: exactly INVEST/PURSUE/WAIT/PASS as defined above. `priority`, `reason` and `next_action` explain urgency and the concrete action. `suggested_amount` must be positive for INVEST and zero otherwise. A zero-amount INVEST is downgraded to WAIT; scoring must never substitute the investor's typical ticket for a missing proposed cheque. `sizing_reason` explains the cheque. `economics` is attractive/unattractive/unknown; `evidence` is sufficient/incomplete; `fit` is compatible/incompatible/unknown. `assessment_summary` contains investment_case, supporting_evidence, counterarguments and decision_logic, preferably 25–80 words each and 140–260 total.
- `category_scores`: all seven factor keys, finite 0–5 or null. Prefer provisional numeric ratings when stage, business model and evidence allow reasoned judgment. Null / `unknown` is reserved for the rare factor with no responsible basis; explain why. `category_confidence` is the internal compatibility name for Evidence strength, with high/medium/low/unknown. Unknown implies null score. `category_drivers` and `category_rationales` use the same keys. Missing disclosure alone is not a reason to lower or omit a factor score.
- `deal_snapshot`: company_summary, product, customer, stage, instrument, terms and market_timing; optional as_of and report_note.
- `diligence_issues`: up to five objects with unique id, integer priority, status (fatal/blocker/risk/resolved), evidence_state (verified/reported/unknown/conflicting), title, finding, decision_impact, evidence_needed, reconsideration_condition, source_ids, resolution_type (document/call/business_milestone/monitor), and resolution_horizon. A monitoring risk cannot also be a blocker.
- `questions`: question, priority, audience (founder/syndicate_lead/counsel/customer), optional boolean, issue_id and evidence_requested. At most three nonoptional founder/company questions.
- `economic_ranges`: {} when unsupported; otherwise currency, illustrative post-money entry valuation bounds, dilution/fee/carry bounds, fee_treatment, carry_basis, target_net_multiple and rationale. The CLI derives lower/midpoint/higher required distributable equity exits. These ranges do not prove an exit is attainable.
- `return_assumptions`: {} when a full model is unavailable. Otherwise include investor currency, valuation currency, FX, post-money entry valuation, distributable-equity exit basis, fees, carry, dilution, no follow-on, years, ownership_note and exclusions. A personal ticket range is required for sizing, not for cheque-independent economics.
- `return_scenarios`: [] when unavailable; otherwise one loss, bear, base and upside scenario with fractional probabilities summing to one. Loss has zero recovery and positive probability. These probabilities are subjective stress assumptions, not forecasts.
- `citations` / `web_sweep_sources`: dated, claim-linked sources with source/title, publication date, access date, URL where applicable and note. Never guess dates or count repeated company claims as independent evidence.
- `assessment_process`: actual booleans for rubric use, web sweep, reconciliation and return modeling, plus notes. Explain process gaps in assessment_limitations.

Derived outputs include weighted_score, score_coverage, effective_weights, attention_flag/reason, economic_sensitivity and optional net returns. `pursuit_recommendation`, `commitment_readiness` and assessment mode are legacy compatibility fields; they are omitted from version 4 serialized assessments.

## Archives

Versions 1–3 remain readable with their original schema and labels. Do not reinterpret an archived INVEST as PURSUE or rewrite version 3 CONSIDER as WAIT; preserve its historical meaning when regenerating reports. Show that no new version 4 recommendation was made for archived records. Version 2 retains its original attention heuristic. Rebuilt reports identify the source schema version.
