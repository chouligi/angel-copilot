# Fictional Assessment Example

All companies, evidence and terms below are invented. The example illustrates screening, not a live recommendation. The rubric and reporting contract govern the rules.

## Fictional Northstar Robotics

### Company Overview

Northstar Robotics develops industrial inspection robots for food manufacturers. Its prototype is intended to automate recurring inspection work in food-production facilities. The fictional packet reports two paid pilots; it does not yet establish repeat production orders. The available material does not explain whether ongoing revenue would come from robot sales, subscriptions or service contracts. [D1]

### Decision Brief

**Weighted score: 3.5 / 5**

Score coverage: 100% · Provisional Unit Economics judgment reflects the capital-heavy deployment model; Low evidence strength reflects missing cost and margin validation. Descriptive score; not a return prediction.

**PURSUE · Priority: High · Attention: Yes**

A focused inspection product, relevant founders and promising paid-pilot signals justify a founder meeting at the stated price. The unanswered production-cost question materially affects the investment case, so prioritize it before expanding diligence.

Attention: High-priority PURSUE; a coherent customer problem and early use justify focused follow-up.

Commitment readiness: Diligence required. Fully loaded delivered economics and repeat-order evidence need verification.

Next action: Request a delivered unit-cost schedule and arrange a pilot-customer reference call.

Industrial inspection robots for food manufacturers · Seed · €12m post-money SAFE cap · Fictional evidence as of October 2026.

#### Investment Case and Decision

**Investment case.** Recurring inspection work gives the company a specific customer problem and a plausible route to revenue. Relevant robotics experience and an operating prototype support the team's ability to explore that opportunity.

**Evidence supporting the case.** Two reported paid pilots and a supplied prototype demonstration provide early signals appropriate to this stage. Collections and customer outcomes have not been independently corroborated; they remain reported evidence rather than proven repeatability.

**Counterarguments and risks.** Installation, service and warranty costs are absent, and pilots may not become repeat orders. Incumbents could respond. Scaled manufacturing and a mature moat remain future execution risks rather than missing-document prerequisites for every seed investment.

**Why this recommendation; what could change it.** PURSUE reflects the value of focused diligence, not permission to commit. Start with the cost schedule and customer reference because they can reveal whether the product creates customer value with plausible delivery margins. Unattractive delivered economics would weaken pursuit priority.

### Seven-Factor Assessment

| Category | Weight | Score / 5 | Evidence strength | Key driver |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Team | 25% | 4.1 | Medium | Relevant robotics experience; manufacturing leadership remains unproven |
| Market | 20% | 3.8 | Medium | Recurring inspection need; buyer budgets and adoption remain uncertain |
| Product | 15% | 3.5 | Low | Prototype demonstrated; customer-side reliability results remain incomplete |
| Traction | 15% | 2.8 | Low | Two reported paid pilots; repeat orders not established |
| Unit Economics | 10% | 3.2 | Low | Paid pilots suggest value; production cost and service margin remain uncertain |
| Defensibility | 10% | 3.1 | Low | Workflow integration may help; switching advantage remains unproven |
| Terms | 5% | 3.0 | Low | Headline cap known; conversion and investor costs need review |

Evidence strength: High = key claims independently corroborated by authoritative evidence; Medium = some corroboration, with material gaps; Low = evidence is mostly reported, incomplete or conflicting; Unknown = evidence quality could not be assessed. Unknown is not a negative score.

Category notes explain the supplied evidence and limitations without treating missing cost data alone as poor economics. Paid pilots and recurring inspection work support plausible early economics, while production cost and service margin remain unproven. The score clears the Pursue eligibility floor, but the verdict still requires a specific case for diligence. Scores are descriptive judgments, not calibrated success probabilities.

### Decision-Critical Diligence

1. **Delivered economics (blocker):** absent installation, warranty and service costs prevent understanding the price-to-margin case. Request an all-in delivered unit-cost schedule. Resolution: document · During initial diligence. Reassess after reviewing contribution margin and cash needs.
2. **Customer value (blocker):** reported pilots are not independent proof of useful performance. Arrange a pilot-customer reference. Resolution: call · During initial diligence. Reassess after confirmation of measured value and repeat-order intent.
3. **Production scaling (risk):** serial manufacturing remains ahead. Resolution: business_milestone · Over the next financing cycle. Review the costed plan now, accepting stage-appropriate scale uncertainty rather than demanding completed mass production.

### Top Questions for the Founder

1. Can you share the fully loaded cost of a delivered unit? Evidence: cost schedule including installation, service and warranty. Linked issue 1.
2. Can you introduce a pilot customer to discuss measured results and intended next orders? Evidence: customer reference call. Linked issue 2.

### Deal Economics

A range-based return sensitivity is unavailable because delivered economics and conversion/cost terms have not been supplied. No exit probabilities or personal cheque are invented. This does not itself prevent PURSUE.
