# Fictional Assessment Example

This illustration is entirely fictional. Names, amounts, documents and sources are invented. Follow the rubric and reporting contract if this example conflicts with either.

## Fictional Northstar Robotics

**Deal snapshot** · Seed · €12m post-money SAFE cap · Industrial inspection robots for food manufacturers

**Decision: WAIT** · €0 now · Reassess after a customer contract bridge and complete unit-cost schedule are independently checked.

The product addresses a costly, recurring inspection task and the team has relevant robotics experience. A credible prototype and two paid pilots support further diligence. The current evidence does not establish repeat orders, fully loaded deployment margins or a reliable production schedule, so the company cannot yet support the proposed price.

### Decision-critical issues

1. **Pilot conversion:** the two pilots do not establish repeat production demand. Verify signed orders, quantities, delivery dates and payment terms.
2. **Delivered margin:** the stated hardware cost excludes installation, field service and warranty. Rebuild margin using full deployment costs.
3. **Production readiness:** the prototype has not completed an independent reliability test or a documented serial-production run.

### Top founder questions

1. Can you share the signed customer orders and a bridge from each pilot to the next paid deployment? This establishes whether pilot interest converts to repeat demand.
2. What is the fully loaded gross margin on a deployed unit, including installation, support and warranty? Please include the underlying cost schedule.
3. Which independent reliability test covers uptime, failure rates and service intervals, and can we speak with the test customer?
